# Notes → Flashcards + Quiz

## SEC-00_LESSON_ID

- **Lesson ID:** M2-L2
- **Lesson Title:** Notes → Flashcards + Quiz
- **Difficulty:** Beginner-friendly
- **Time:** 18 minutes

---

## SEC-01_SKILL_OUTCOME

You can derive **flashcards and quiz questions** from *your* notes without smuggling AI answers into closed assessments.

---

## SEC-02_WHAT_YOU_WILL_BUILD

One tangible artifact for your current semester (notes page, outline, deck skeleton, test list, or dashboard section) saved as `M2-L2-output.md` or screenshots in your workspace.

---

## SEC-03_TOOL_FOCUS

Claude/Gemini-type assistant + handwritten notes photo (if allowed)

---

## SEC-04_WHAT_THIS_TOOL_DOES

Turn noise into routines: notes, quizzes, pacing. AI helps **structure**. You supply **truth**.

Tools here **accelerate scaffolding and critique**. They **never** certify truth. Policies differ by campus — **Verify** syllabus language. Facts that matter for grades get a second source (textbook page, paper DOI, official doc).

---

## SEC-05_WHEN_TO_USE

- You feel slow because the task is **messy**, not because you are “bad.”
- You already have **raw material** (PDF, lecture photo, half draft).
- You will still **rewrite in your voice** after AI helps.

---

## SEC-06_WHEN_NOT_TO_USE

- The assignment forbids AI **or** requires a closed-book exam mimic.
- You are tempted to paste **questions you must solve alone**.
- Uploading materials would leak **privacy** or **copyright** — check first.

---

## SEC-07_SETUP_IN_2_MINUTES

1. Open your permitted assistant in a browser you trust.
2. Pin two tabs: **this lesson** + **official tool help** (*Verify pricing & data rules*).
3. Start a scratch note titled `M2-L2-draft`.

## SEC-08_BAD_WORKFLOW

You demand slides Friday night without storyline first.

---

## SEC-09_BAD_RESULT_EXAMPLE

> Deck is 62 bullets of sedation.

---

## SEC-10_WHY_IT_FAILED

Usually: **skipped verification**, **skipped policy**, **skipped personal rewrite**. Any one collapses marks or trust fast.

---

## SEC-11_GOOD_WORKFLOW

1. From your note, mark **atomic facts** (one idea per card).
2. Ask AI for **question stems only** first — no answers.
3. Attempt answers cold — then reveal your note section.
4. For wrong cards, rewrite the back **in your voice**.
5. Cap new cards/day (e.g. 15) — avoid deck bloat.

---

## SEC-12_COPY_PASTE_PROMPTS

### Prompt A
```txt
From my note (paste), create 12 Anki-style cards: Front = question. Back = blank in your output — I will fill backs myself offline.
Actually: output only fronts + tag [topic].
```

### Prompt B
```txt
Now I paste my fronts + my draft backs (unfinished). Find 3 backs that are too long — suggest shorter phrasing without changing meaning.
```

### Prompt C
```txt
Generate 6 **application** questions (scenario) from my note — no solutions.
```

---

## SEC-13_GOOD_RESULT_EXAMPLE

> 12-slide arc with presenter notes conversational.

---

## SEC-14_PRACTICE_TASK

12 fronts only from Prompt A. Fill 6 backs offline. Grade yourself.

---

## SEC-15_CHALLENGE_TASK

48-hour revisit: redo same fronts. Count how many you fail — tune cards.

## SEC-17_COMMON_MISTAKES

1. Cards with paragraphs — you will never review.
2. Putting exam-prohibited AI solutions on back.
3. Building deck before notes exist.
4. Thousands of passive recognition cards.
5. Sharing decks with unethical full exam dumps.

---

## SEC-18_WHAT_YOU_LEARNED

- **Fronts-first** builds honest recall.
- Brevity wins spaced repetition.
- Scenarios beat trivia when allowed.
