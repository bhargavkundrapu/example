# Find Sources Fast

## SEC-00_LESSON_ID

- **Lesson ID:** M4-L1
- **Lesson Title:** Find Sources Fast
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Time:** 15 minutes

---

## SEC-01_SKILL_OUTCOME

You can **triage sources** into trust tiers and open only what you will actually read.

---

## SEC-02_WHAT_YOU_WILL_BUILD

One tangible artifact for your current semester (notes page, outline, deck skeleton, test list, or dashboard section) saved as `M4-L1-output.md` or screenshots in your workspace.

---

## SEC-03_TOOL_FOCUS

ChatGPT-type assistant + textbook PDF

---

## SEC-04_WHAT_THIS_TOOL_DOES

Citation discipline beats panic citations. Teach tools to amplify **sources you possess**.

Tools here **accelerate scaffolding and critique**. They **never** certify truth. Policies differ by campus — **Verify** syllabus language. Facts that matter for grades get a second source (textbook page, paper DOI, official doc).

---

## SEC-05_WHEN_TO_USE

- You feel slow because the task is **messy**, not because you are “bad.”
- You already have **raw material** (PDF, lecture photo, half draft).
- You will still **rewrite in your voice** after AI helps.

---

## SEC-06_WHEN_NOT_TO_USE

- The assignment forbids AI **or** requires a closed-book exam mimic.
- You are tempted to paste **questions you must solve alone**.
- Uploading materials would leak **privacy** or **copyright** — check first.

---

## SEC-07_SETUP_IN_2_MINUTES

1. Open your permitted assistant in a browser you trust.
2. Pin two tabs: **this lesson** + **official tool help** (*Verify pricing & data rules*).
3. Start a scratch note titled `M4-L1-draft`.

---

## SEC-08_BAD_WORKFLOW

You ask for diagram labels without checking units. Poster judges notice.

---

## SEC-09_BAD_RESULT_EXAMPLE

> Axis labelled “Stuff vs Things.” Grade: friendly chaos.

---

## SEC-10_WHY_IT_FAILED

Usually: **skipped verification**, **skipped policy**, **skipped personal rewrite**. Any one collapses marks or trust fast.

---

## SEC-11_GOOD_WORKFLOW

1. Write your question in one sentence.
2. Search library DB + one general index you trust.
3. Skim abstract/intro + limitations.
4. Keep 3–7 sources max in a working table (year, method, why kept).
5. AI helps sort table — not invent authors.

---

## SEC-12_COPY_PASTE_PROMPTS

### Prompt A
```txt
My research question (one line): ___. Suggest search keywords + 2 inclusion filters + 2 exclusion filters. No fake paper titles.
```

### Prompt B
```txt
I paste metadata lines (title/year). Classify: KEEP / MAYBE / DROP with one reason each — conservative bias.
```

### Prompt C
```txt
Turn KEEP list into a reading order (easy → hard) with time estimates I can edit.
```

---

## SEC-13_GOOD_RESULT_EXAMPLE

> Axes cite measured units + caption states data source CSV name.

---

## SEC-14_PRACTICE_TASK

Question → 5 sources triaged → table of 3 KEEP.

---

## SEC-15_CHALLENGE_TASK

Read one KEEP abstract blind — predict method; compare.

## SEC-17_COMMON_MISTAKES

1. Trusting citations AI “remembers”. **Verify.**
2. Uploading ID cards “for OCR fun.” Never.
3. Skipping the rewrite step.
4. Mixing teammates’ unpublished work.
5. Confusing polish tools with originality.

---

## SEC-18_WHAT_YOU_LEARNED

- Fewer, better sources.
- Tables beat infinite tabs.
- AI sorts, you **own** keep/drop.
