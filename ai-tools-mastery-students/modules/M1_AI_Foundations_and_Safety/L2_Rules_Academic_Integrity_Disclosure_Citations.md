## SEC-04_WHAT_THIS_TOOL_DOES

This module anchors **honesty** and **verification** — the premium advantage is finishing faster **without** fear.

Tools here **accelerate scaffolding and critique**. They **never** certify truth. Policies differ by campus — **Verify** syllabus language. Facts that matter for grades get a second source (textbook page, paper DOI, official doc).

## SEC-11_GOOD_WORKFLOW

1. Open syllabus + honour code PDF (bookmark them).
2. Highlight verbs: prohibited / allowed-with-disclosure / unclear.
3. Write three sentences **in your words**: What I submit as mine. What helpers I may use. What I must cite.
4. Draft a disclosure line stub (adapt later per assignment).
5. Ask AI ONLY to shorten grammar — not invent policy facts.
6. Email TA one **yes/no** question if rules are vague (**Verify** interpretation).

## SEC-12_COPY_PASTE_PROMPTS

### Prompt A
```txt
I will paste my college’s short AI/honour policy text (under 500 words). Summarise in 5 bullets: must-do / must-not / gray area / ask instructor / citation reminder.
If text is missing, say [NEED_SYLLABUS] — do not invent rules.
```

### Prompt B
```txt
Here is MY summary of the policy in my own words (paste next). Check: did I miss a prohibited use? Flag only if grounded in my pasted policy text.
```

### Prompt C
```txt
Give 3 **safe** ways to describe AI use in an assignment footnote (examples only). Do not claim any are required — I will match my rubric.
```

## SEC-17_COMMON_MISTAKES

1. Copying a friend’s disclosure line — your process differs.
2. Letting AI invent “typical college policy.” **Verify** only your doc.
3. Hiding AI use when syllabus asks clarity — risk > reward.
4. Confusing “I used Grammarly” with “I used AI to generate methods.”
5. Skipping citation for paraphrased sources.
