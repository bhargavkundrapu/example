# Project Story in One Line

> ✅ **Quick Win:** One line should answer: who benefits + what pain + what you built—truthfully.

---

## SEC-00_LESSON_ID

- **Lesson ID:** `M4-L1`
- **Lesson Title:** Project Story in One Line
- **Difficulty:** Beginner-friendly
- **Time:** 15–22 minutes

---

## SEC-01_SKILL_OUTCOME

Deliver a clear one-line project headline you can defend when the interview starts hot.

---

## SEC-02_WHY_THIS_MATTERS

Many students begin with stack dump. A headline buys you 10 seconds of attention to guide the listener.

Also: it forces you to know your real user and problem.

---

## SEC-03_REAL_INTERVIEW_SCENARIO

**“So… what’s your project?”** in a noisy hall interview.

---

## SEC-04_FRAMEWORK_YOU_WILL_USE

**Name:** Headline Line (USER + PAIN + OUTCOME + YOUR LANE)

**Definition:** A single sentence map—like a movie logline for your project (no fake awards).

---

## SEC-05_BAD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

> “We used React Node Mongo JWT Docker microservices AI…”

---

## SEC-06_WHAT_WENT_WRONG

- Stack soup before problem.
- No user/pain clarity.
- Sounds performative.

---

## SEC-07_GOOD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

> “We built a campus lost-and-found tracker that cuts admin message chaos—my lane was item matching + simple admin dashboard; not full security hardening.”

---

## SEC-08_BREAKDOWN_LINE_BY_LINE

- User/pain first.
- Outcome plain language.
- Lane boundary shows maturity.

---

## SEC-09_SHORT_VERSION

**Template:** We built `_____` for `_____` to reduce `_____`—I owned `_____`.

---

## SEC-10_LONG_VERSION

Add second line only if asked: scope boundary + honest limitations.

---

## SEC-11_FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS


1. **Q:** "Why this problem?"
   **A:** Real observed pain + constraints—no fake ‘market research’ unless true.


1. **Q:** "Novelty?"
   **A:** Be careful: student projects often overlap; honesty > hype.


1. **Q:** "Production?"
   **A:** Clarify deployment truth; VERIFY hosting numbers.


---

## SEC-12_CONFIDENCE_DELIVERY_TIPS

- **Voice:** speak clearly; emphasize names and truthful numbers slowly.
- **Pace:** slow down on technical words you can explain.
- **Pauses:** short silence beats extra “umm/like”.
- **Body / camera:** relaxed shoulders; stable camera; hands visible briefly.

---

## SEC-13_PRACTICE_DRILL

Write **10 one-liners** for the same project (different emphasis). Pick best 2.

---

## SEC-14_AI_MOCK_PRACTICE

### Prompt A (simulate interviewer ethically)

Ask "What is your project—one line?" then stress-test with cynicism. Rules: communication practice ONLY. Forbidden: cheating on tests/assignments, feeding hidden answers during live assessments. Do not invent my achievements—ask factual follow-ups.

### Prompt B (structure + feedback)

Score headline clarity vs stack spam tendency. Rules: critique clarity, structure, honesty, pacing, confidence. Forbidden: rewriting my story with fabricated facts. Label anything unverifiable as VERIFY.

---

## SEC-15_SELF_EVALUATION_RUBRIC

| Dimension | 1–5 | What you're measuring |
|-----------|-----|-----------------------|
| Clarity | — | Listener can repeat your headline in one sentence |
| Structure | — | Moves are sequential, not scrambled |
| Confidence | — | Calm pacing, recovery after slips |

_Score 1–5 on each row. Pick your lowest score and rewrite one paragraph only._

---

## SEC-16_COMMON_MISTAKES

- Claiming ‘first in world’ without evidence.
- Confusing demo with full product maturity.
- Hiding that it was a graded mini scope.
- Using buzzwords you cannot define.
- Blurring individual vs team ownership.

---

## SEC-17_WHAT_YOU_LEARNED

- Headline steers the interview.
- Boundaries build trust.
- Problem language > tool language first.
