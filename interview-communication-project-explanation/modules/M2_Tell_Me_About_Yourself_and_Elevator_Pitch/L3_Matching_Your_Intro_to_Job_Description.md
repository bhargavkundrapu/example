# Matching Your Intro to Job Description

> ⚠️ **Common Mistake:** Keyword stuffing sounds fake. Match with *examples*, not synonyms spam.

---

## SEC-00_LESSON_ID

- **Lesson ID:** `M2-L3`
- **Lesson Title:** Matching Your Intro to Job Description
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **Time:** 17–24 minutes

---

## SEC-01_SKILL_OUTCOME

Tailor your intro ethically: align to job themes using truthful evidence and clean bridges.

---

## SEC-02_WHY_THIS_MATTERS

Interviewers want relevance. If you sound random, they assume you applied blindly.

Alignment is not lying. It is highlighting the true overlap.

---

## SEC-03_REAL_INTERVIEW_SCENARIO

You applied to a role mentioning **APIs**, **testing**, and **documentation**. The interviewer asks:

**“Why this role?”** right after your intro.

---

## SEC-04_FRAMEWORK_YOU_WILL_USE

**Name:** JD Mirror (TRUTH + EXAMPLE + BRIDGE)

**Definition:** Pick 2–3 JD themes you can defend with real work, name them plainly, then bridge with one example each.

---

## SEC-05_BAD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

_Bad example (fiction):_

> “I am perfect for APIs because I love APIs and I also love testing and documentation is my passion and I read your website and I am aligned with your culture and values…”

---

## SEC-06_WHAT_WENT_WRONG

- Claims without proof sound like marketing.
- ‘Culture’ talk without specifics can feel empty (VERIFY what you’re allowed to reference).
- No concrete artifact mapping to JD lines.

---

## SEC-07_GOOD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

_Good pattern:_

> “Two overlaps I can defend with real work: first, I built a small REST module for an attendance feature—wrote happy-path tests first after clarifying edge cases. Second, I documented request/response examples for teammates because handoffs broke once. If you want, I’ll map those to your ‘reliability + internal customers’ line next.”

---

## SEC-08_BREAKDOWN_LINE_BY_LINE

- Names **two** JD themes—not ten.
- Each theme gets a **micro-story**.
- Offers mapping permission—shows research without sucking up.

---

## SEC-09_SHORT_VERSION

**30–45 seconds bridge:** JD theme → your proof sentence → polite offer to deepen.

---

## SEC-10_LONG_VERSION

**Longer variant:** third theme ONLY if truthful and JD explicitly lists it.

---

## SEC-11_FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS


1. **Q:** "Which JD line matters most?"
   **A:** Ask them lightly—then prioritize your strongest evidence.


1. **Q:** "Ever used our product?"
   **A:** Tell truth—if limited exposure, explain what you did try and VERIFY details.


1. **Q:** "What's your weakest overlap?"
   **A:** Honest gap + honest learning path beats bluff.


---

## SEC-12_CONFIDENCE_DELIVERY_TIPS

- **Voice:** speak clearly; emphasize names and truthful numbers slowly.
- **Pace:** slow down on technical words you can explain.
- **Pauses:** short silence beats extra “umm/like”.
- **Body / camera:** relaxed shoulders; stable camera; hands visible briefly.

---

## SEC-13_PRACTICE_DRILL

**Highlighter drill:** Print/screenshot JD. Highlight 3 lines. Write 6 lines tying each highlight to ONE repo/lab artifact. Delete any line you cannot defend.

---

## SEC-14_AI_MOCK_PRACTICE

### Prompt A (simulate interviewer ethically)

You are hiring manager. Paste JD summary, then ask "Why this role?" after my intro. Rules: communication practice ONLY. Forbidden: cheating on tests/assignments, feeding hidden answers during live assessments. Do not invent my achievements—ask factual follow-ups.

### Prompt B (structure + feedback)

Judge whether my JD mapping uses evidence vs puffery. Suggest tighter bridges. Rules: critique clarity, structure, honesty, pacing, confidence. Forbidden: rewriting my story with fabricated facts. Label anything unverifiable as VERIFY.

---

## SEC-15_SELF_EVALUATION_RUBRIC

| Dimension | 1–5 | What you're measuring |
|-----------|-----|-----------------------|
| Clarity | — | Listener can repeat your headline in one sentence |
| Structure | — | Moves are sequential, not scrambled |
| Confidence | — | Calm pacing, recovery after slips |

_Score 1–5 on each row. Pick your lowest score and rewrite one paragraph only._

---

## SEC-16_COMMON_MISTAKES

- Copy-pasting JD phrases with no lived example.
- Pretending deep domain knowledge day one.
- Mentioning confidential past employer specifics—VIOLATION risk.
- Overclaiming tools ("expert") for one tutorial exposure.
- Forgetting to ask what success looks like in first 30–60 days (verify appropriateness).

---

## SEC-17_WHAT_YOU_LEARNED

- Match with stories, not synonyms.
- Two strong overlaps beat six weak ones.
- Honest gaps + learning plan can still pass if role is trainee-friendly.
