# Common Intro Mistakes and Fixes

> ✅ **Quick Win:** If you fix only one thing—remove the ‘random fact pile’ and add one proof object.

## SEC-01_SKILL_OUTCOME

Diagnose common intro failure patterns and swap them with safer high-trust lines.

---

## SEC-02_WHY_THIS_MATTERS

Most students don’t fail because they are ‘bad.’ They fail because they repeat the same pattern under pressure.

Naming patterns gives you control.

---

## SEC-03_REAL_INTERVIEW_SCENARIO

You delivered an intro. The interviewer looks neutral and asks:

**“Okay—what’s your biggest strength?”** but you already said ‘hardworking’ twice.

---

## SEC-04_FRAMEWORK_YOU_WILL_USE

**Name:** Mistake → Fix Card (NAME → REPLACE → REHEARSE)

**Definition:** Name the failure pattern, replace with a better line, rehearse three clean takes.

---

## SEC-05_BAD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

_Bad example (fiction):_

> “Strength is hard work and smart work and leadership and communication and problem solving…”

---

## SEC-06_WHAT_WENT_WRONG

- Strength list with no proof repeats intro mistake.
- ‘Smart work’ cliché unless defined with example.
- Too many abstract nouns stacked.

---

## SEC-07_GOOD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

_Good repair pattern:_

> “I'll make it concrete: I’m disciplined at turning messy specs into checklists—example: ticket app feature where ‘urgent’ was vague; I wrote a triage rule with the team and cut duplicate tickets. If you want another strength, I can give a second story.”

---

## SEC-08_BREAKDOWN_LINE_BY_LINE

- Chooses **one** strength label tied to behavior.
- Gives **example** with before/after clarity.
- Offers second story—signals depth without rambling now.

---

## SEC-09_SHORT_VERSION

**Fix template:** One strength = one behavior + one example + offer second story.

---

## SEC-10_LONG_VERSION

**Long version:** add second story only if asked; keep each story STAR-shaped.

---

## SEC-11_FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS


1. **Q:** "Another strength?"
   **A:** Pick a different dimension (collaboration vs technical).


1. **Q:** "That strength is common."
   **A:** Add a crisp differentiator: tool, constraint, metric (VERIFY).


1. **Q:** "Prove it fast."
   **A:** Give 20-second micro-story with your action verbs.


---

## SEC-12_CONFIDENCE_DELIVERY_TIPS

- **Voice:** speak clearly; emphasize names and truthful numbers slowly.
- **Pace:** slow down on technical words you can explain.
- **Pauses:** short silence beats extra “umm/like”.
- **Body / camera:** relaxed shoulders; stable camera; hands visible briefly.

---

## SEC-13_PRACTICE_DRILL

**Mistake zoo:** Write 6 bad lines you used before. For each, write replacement + read aloud once.

---

## SEC-14_AI_MOCK_PRACTICE

### Prompt A (simulate interviewer ethically)

Press me on vague strengths after my intro. Rules: communication practice ONLY. Forbidden: cheating on tests/assignments, feeding hidden answers during live assessments. Do not invent my achievements—ask factual follow-ups.

### Prompt B (structure + feedback)

List vague words in my transcript and propose concrete replacements—no new fake wins. Rules: critique clarity, structure, honesty, pacing, confidence. Forbidden: rewriting my story with fabricated facts. Label anything unverifiable as VERIFY.

## SEC-15_SELF_EVALUATION_RUBRIC

| Dimension | 1–5 | What you're measuring |
|-----------|-----|-----------------------|
| Clarity | — | Listener can repeat your headline in one sentence |
| Structure | — | Moves are sequential, not scrambled |
| Confidence | — | Calm pacing, recovery after slips |

_Score 1–5 on each row. Pick your lowest score and rewrite one paragraph only._

---

## SEC-16_COMMON_MISTAKES

- Recycling the same strength story for every question.
- Bragging without listening cues.
- Comparing yourself to other candidates by name/rank.
- Overusing ‘always/never’ absolutes.
- Arguing when corrected—especially on facts you should verify.

---

## SEC-17_WHAT_YOU_LEARNED

- Concrete beats abstract in strength answers.
- Pattern naming builds self-coaching.
- One proof can travel across questions if trimmed.
