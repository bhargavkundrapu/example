# 30 Second Introduction Template

> ✅ **Quick Win:** First sentence = who you are academically + what you want next.

---

## SEC-00_LESSON_ID

- **Lesson ID:** `M2-L1`
- **Lesson Title:** 30 Second Introduction Template
- **Difficulty:** Beginner-friendly
- **Time:** 15–22 minutes

---

## SEC-01_SKILL_OUTCOME

Deliver a truthful 30-second introduction that sounds organized and ends with a clean handoff question.

---

## SEC-02_WHY_THIS_MATTERS

Interviewers need fast orientation. If you ramble, they work harder—and you feel more nervous.

A short intro is not ‘small talk.’ It is professional navigation.

---

## SEC-03_REAL_INTERVIEW_SCENARIO

Remote interview. The interviewer says:

**“We have 30 minutes—start with you. Keep it crisp.”**

---

## SEC-04_FRAMEWORK_YOU_WILL_USE

**Name:** 30s Intro Bridge (WHO + PROOF + AIM + HANDOFF)

**Definition:** Your intro is a map: identity, one proof, aim, then a polite choice for the interviewer.

---

## SEC-05_BAD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

_Bad example (fiction):_

> “Hi… I'm from a small town… I love sports… I learned many languages… I did many projects… I'm very passionate… I like challenges… sorry I'm nervous…”

---

## SEC-06_WHAT_WENT_WRONG

- No clear academic identity early (degree/branch/focus).
- No proof object (what you built, tested, or studied deeply).
- Adjectives replace evidence (passionate / hardworking).
- Apology loop becomes the story.
- No handoff—so the interviewer must guess what to ask next.

---

## SEC-07_GOOD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

_Good pattern (replace with your truth):_

> “Hi—I'm Arjun, final-year Civil Engineering student with a software side project habit. I built a simple site for tracking lab equipment requests—I owned the forms + database schema in my scope. Seeking internships that mix practical field thinking with disciplined documentation. Want product story or coursework next?”

---

## SEC-08_BREAKDOWN_LINE_BY_LINE

- **WHO:** degree + truthful focus line.
- **PROOF:** one bounded artifact with your lane claimed clearly.
- **AIM:** role family language without pretending you fit every stack.
- **HANDOFF:** polite choice—not an interrogation.

---

## SEC-09_SHORT_VERSION

_30–40 seconds:_

```
Hi—I'm _____ (degree + discipline).
Proof: _____ (project/lab)—I owned _____ .
Seeking _____ (honest).
Prefer project depth or coursework—what helps you?
```

Only use facts you can verify. If unsure, say VERIFY offline.

---

## SEC-10_LONG_VERSION

_If expanded to ~55–70 seconds:_ add **one coursework anchor** + **one learning habit** (“I revise with checklists”), still no fabricated awards.

---

## SEC-11_FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS


1. **Q:** "Why Civil + software?"
   **A:** Only if true: bridges field documentation needs with lighter tools—I keep examples truthful and narrow.


1. **Q:** "Sounds generic."
   **A:** I can sharpen with one concrete module—tell me JD keywords so I anchor honestly without stuffing buzzwords.


1. **Q:** "What do you want in 5 years?"
   **A:** Keep it exploratory: strengthen engineering fundamentals + teamwork—avoid fantasy titles. VERIFY wording with mentor if needed.


---

## SEC-12_CONFIDENCE_DELIVERY_TIPS

- **Voice:** speak clearly; emphasize names and truthful numbers slowly.
- **Pace:** slow down on technical words you can explain.
- **Pauses:** short silence beats extra “umm/like”.
- **Body / camera:** relaxed shoulders; stable camera; hands visible briefly.
- Smile small at greeting; exhale silently before sentence one.

---

## SEC-13_PRACTICE_DRILL

**Shrink-and-speak:** Write 140 words → cut to 85 → speak 3 times; track fillers; stop when under 34 seconds.

|-----------|-----|-----------------------|
| Clarity | — | Listener can repeat your headline in one sentence |
| Structure | — | Moves are sequential, not scrambled |
| Confidence | — | Calm pacing, recovery after slips |

_Score 1–5 on each row. Pick your lowest score and rewrite one paragraph only._

---

## SEC-16_COMMON_MISTAKES

- Starting from childhood unless it directly supports relocation/disability narrative you chose to disclose.
- Buzzword stacking (‘AI/ML/DevOps’) without truthful depth.
- Memorizing a script that mismatches nervous voice—sounds unnatural.
- Name-dropping internships you did not complete.
- Forgetting handshake equivalent online: polite greeting + simple thank-you.

---

## SEC-17_WHAT_YOU_LEARNED

- Proof beats adjectives in the first impression window.
- A choice question ends the intro cleanly.
- Integrity language builds trust.
