# STAR Framework Simple

> ⚠️ **Common Mistake:** Long Situation, tiny Action—flip the ratio when possible.

---

## SEC-00_LESSON_ID

- **Lesson ID:** `M3-L2`
- **Lesson Title:** STAR Framework Simple
- **Difficulty:** Beginner-friendly
- **Time:** 16–24 minutes

---

## SEC-01_SKILL_OUTCOME

Deliver a truthful STAR answer in 60–90 seconds with strong Action verbs.

---

## SEC-02_WHY_THIS_MATTERS

STAR is not a school essay. It is a backpack for listeners to carry your story without getting lost.

---

## SEC-03_REAL_INTERVIEW_SCENARIO

**“Tell me about a time you solved a tough bug.”**

---

## SEC-04_FRAMEWORK_YOU_WILL_USE

**Name:** STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result)

**Definition:** A story spine: tight context, your job, what *you* did, what changed (+ learning if needed).

---

## SEC-05_BAD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

> “There was a bug… it was hard… eventually it worked… teamwork… learning… lots of debugging…”

---

## SEC-06_WHAT_WENT_WRONG

- No crisp situation boundary.
- No clear task ownership.
- Actions not listed—so no trust.
- Result vague.

---

## SEC-07_GOOD_ANSWER_TRANSCRIPT

> “S: Feature broke after a dependency update—login failed for some users.

T: I owned tracing it before release cut.

A: reproduced with a minimal account, bisected commits, added logging around token refresh, wrote a regression test for the edge timeout.

R: login stable on test devices; PR merged with review notes. Learning: pin versions + checklist for auth changes.”

---

## SEC-08_BREAKDOWN_LINE_BY_LINE

- Keeps Situation short.
- Task names your mandate.
- Action is sequential and inspectable.
- Result ties to observable outcome + learning.

---

## SEC-09_SHORT_VERSION

**60s STAR:** 2–3 situation lines, 1 task line, 3–5 action lines, 2 result lines.

---

## SEC-10_LONG_VERSION

**2–3 min STAR:** add one stakeholder line + one trade-off + VERIFY metrics if used.

---

## SEC-11_FOLLOW_UP_QUESTIONS


1. **Q:** "What if you weren't the solo owner?"
   **A:** Separate team context, then pinpoint your commits/tests/docs.


1. **Q:** "What test strategy?"
   **A:** Name types you actually ran—not fantasy coverage numbers.


1. **Q:** "Why not rewrite everything?"
   **A:** Discuss trade-offs honestly—time/risk/stakeholders.


---

## SEC-12_CONFIDENCE_DELIVERY_TIPS

- **Voice:** speak clearly; emphasize names and truthful numbers slowly.
- **Pace:** slow down on technical words you can explain.
- **Pauses:** short silence beats extra “umm/like”.
- **Body / camera:** relaxed shoulders; stable camera; hands visible briefly.

---

## SEC-13_PRACTICE_DRILL

Take one story → write **STAR in bullet lines** → speak without looking → compress to **45s**.

---

## SEC-14_AI_MOCK_PRACTICE

### Prompt A (simulate interviewer ethically)

Behavioral interviewer: ask STAR follow-ups only. Rules: communication practice ONLY. Forbidden: cheating on tests/assignments, feeding hidden answers during live assessments. Do not invent my achievements—ask factual follow-ups.

### Prompt B (structure + feedback)

Rebuild my answer as STAR bullet outline; highlight missing Action granularity. Rules: critique clarity, structure, honesty, pacing, confidence. Forbidden: rewriting my story with fabricated facts. Label anything unverifiable as VERIFY.

## SEC-15_SELF_EVALUATION_RUBRIC

| Dimension | 1–5 | What you're measuring |
|-----------|-----|-----------------------|
| Clarity | — | Listener can repeat your headline in one sentence |
| Structure | — | Moves are sequential, not scrambled |
| Confidence | — | Calm pacing, recovery after slips |

_Score 1–5 on each row. Pick your lowest score and rewrite one paragraph only._

---

## SEC-16_COMMON_MISTAKES

- Star Wars plot length Situation.
- Results that are secretly ‘feelings.’
- Stealing credit.
- Forgetting measurable or qualitative observable change.
- Using STAR robotically aloud (“situation comma…”) — keep natural language.

---

## SEC-17_WHAT_YOU_LEARNED

- Action density drives credibility.
- Short situation respects interview time.
- Learning lines can rescue imperfect results—if true.
